01 / DESIGN

Design problem

A healthcare visit involves more than a single appointment. A person may need to decide where to seek care, book an appropriate visit, find the right location, and understand the next step along the way. These tasks can become confusing when they are spread across separate screens, instructions, or services.

Our five-person team developed MediCamp, an interactive prototype that brings several stages of a healthcare visit into a connected user experience. The design explores triage, appointment booking, navigation, and accessibility-oriented interactions from the perspective of the person using the service.

02 / DESIGN

Mapping the experience

We began by examining representative tasks and the decisions required at each step. Personas and task-flow diagrams helped us describe how someone might move from recognizing a need for care to finding an appointment and navigating a visit. These artifacts also made it easier to identify where an interface needed clear options, status information, and continuity between tasks.

The workflow diagrams became a working reference for our information architecture. We used them to organize the screens and establish how users would move between triage, scheduling, and navigation.

03 / DESIGN

From wireframes to an interactive prototype

We translated the task structure into wireframes and then a higher-fidelity Figma prototype. Moving between these representations let us examine the layout of individual screens alongside the sequence of actions a user would take. Critique and iteration informed the organization of information and the interactions between screens.

The final course deliverable was a clickable prototype representing the intended experience. It can be explored through the linked Figma file.

04 / DESIGN

My contribution

As project manager, I coordinated milestones, deliverables, and design documentation across the team. I also contributed to the information architecture, interaction design, task flows, wireframes, and prototype development. The project gave me direct experience connecting design decisions to user tasks and coordinating a shared HCI workflow from early models to an interactive deliverable.
