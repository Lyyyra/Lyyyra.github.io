Charged Dust Dynamics in Exoplanetary Magnetospheres.

Simulating how radiation and electromagnetic forces alter dust-grain orbits.

01 / RESEARCH

The question

Small dust grains around stars experience competing forces. Gravity pulls them inward, stellar radiation pushes outward, and Poynting–Robertson drag gradually changes their orbits. For an electrically charged grain, the stellar magnetic field introduces another influence whose strength and direction depend on the grain's motion and charge. Together, these forces can produce escape, inward migration, or oscillatory trajectories.

For my senior thesis with Professor Phil Arras, I use numerical simulations to investigate how magnetic-field strength, polarity, grain size, and initial conditions change the orbital behavior of charged dust. One question interests me particularly: under what conditions can electromagnetic forces alter a trajectory that would otherwise escape under radiation pressure?

02 / RESEARCH

Building the model

I began by testing numerical integrators and convergence on simpler problems before constructing a simulation workflow using Python and REBOUND. I incorporated additional prescriptions for radiation pressure, Poynting–Robertson drag, and Lorentz forces, then used orbital integrations to follow each particle's position and velocity. My analysis scripts produce trajectory maps, radial time series, and comparisons between simulations with different physical parameters.

Because the field can corotate with the star, the grain's velocity relative to the rotating magnetic field matters. I examine the direction and magnitude of the electric and magnetic contributions, and compare the timescales of rapid oscillations with changes in the orbit over longer intervals.

My analysis scripts organize simulation outputs into comparisons across physical parameters. In addition to the overall shape of an orbit, I examine radial turning points, oscillatory behavior, and timescales associated with the particle's cyclotron motion.

03 / RESEARCH

Running and interpreting experiments

I have compared runs with different field strengths and polarities, including simulations with and without corotation. I also vary grain size and initial orbital radius to see how these choices affect outward motion, inward drift, and repeated radial turning points. Plotting radius against time helps distinguish sustained bounded oscillations from oscillations that accompany an escaping or migrating orbit.

One important revision concerned radiation pressure on nanometer-scale grains. The radiation-pressure efficiency factor, Q_pr, depends on grain size; using a size-dependent model changed the parameter estimates and some of the resulting trajectories. I have also used force-magnitude checks to identify parameter combinations that merit closer examination before interpreting an apparent dynamical effect.

The current phase of the project focuses on initial conditions that produce escape in the radiation-and-gravity baseline and asks how an increasingly strong magnetic field changes that outcome. I am using these experiments to study the transition toward bounded epicyclic motion and to compare the simulations with the physical picture in the literature.

04 / RESEARCH

My contribution

I write and maintain the simulation and analysis code, design parameter sweeps, compare runs, and examine whether the results are consistent with the force prescriptions and relevant physical timescales. Much of the work involves finding the precise reason two trajectories differ, then designing another experiment that isolates the relevant parameter.
