01 / RESEARCH

Three questions, three datasets

In ASTR 4140, I worked on three research projects with different astronomical data sources. Each required its own process for selecting data, checking its quality, building a suitable analysis, and interpreting the measurements in physical terms.

02 / RESEARCH

I / Identifying Sagittarius Stream candidates

The Sagittarius Stream consists of stars left behind by a dwarf galaxy disrupted by the Milky Way. I used SDSS DR16 photometry to search for candidate stream stars and compared the selected region with a control field. After photometric quality cuts and extinction corrections, I constructed color–magnitude diagrams and compared the observed stars with a Dartmouth isochrone. I used the adopted stellar population model to refine the candidate selection and then examined the resulting sky distributions.

I queried SDSS using spatial and photometric-quality criteria, corrected the stellar magnitudes for extinction, and propagated errors into the g−r color index. I then constructed color–magnitude diagrams for the candidate and comparison fields. To refine the selection, I compared the stars with a Dartmouth stellar-population isochrone using an age of 5 Gyr and metallicity [Fe/H] = −1.1, retaining candidates within a two-sigma tolerance around the model sequence.

The before-and-after color–magnitude diagrams make the selection process visible: a broad field population is narrowed to stars whose colors and magnitudes are consistent with the model. The spatial comparison helps show where those candidate stars are concentrated.

03 / RESEARCH

II / Black holes and host galaxies in IllustrisTNG

I analyzed the TNG100-1 simulation at redshift zero, comparing black hole mass with properties of the surrounding halo or galaxy. I used Friends-of-Friends halo measurements and SubFind subhalo quantities, including dark matter mass, stellar mass, and velocity dispersion. For each comparison, I selected valid measurements for logarithmic analysis and fitted scaling relations in log–log space.

For each comparison, I selected positive values for logarithmic analysis and fitted a power-law relation by applying linear regression in log–log space. The halo-scale relation gave a slope of approximately 1.39 with R² ≈ 0.86. The subhalo dark-matter relation was shallower and more dispersed, with a slope near 1.01 and R² ≈ 0.56. The subhalo stellar-mass relation had a slope near 0.77 and R² ≈ 0.83, while the velocity-dispersion relation showed a steeper slope near 3.95 and R² ≈ 0.85.

The different fits revealed how the apparent relationship depends on which host quantity is measured. Comparing halo-scale and subhalo-scale data also made the interpretation of a regression more concrete: data selection and the definitions of the variables shape the slope, scatter, and physical meaning of the result.

04 / RESEARCH

III / Stellar membership and dynamics of NGC 362

I used Gaia DR3 astrometry to study NGC 362, a globular cluster observed against a crowded stellar background. I queried sources around the cluster, applied astrometric and photometric quality cuts, and examined their color–magnitude and proper-motion distributions. I then used a Gaussian mixture model to separate populations in proper-motion space and identify candidate cluster members.

I began with an ADQL query for sources around the cluster, then applied photometric and astrometric quality cuts, including a RUWE threshold of 1.4. I inspected color–magnitude and proper-motion diagrams, used proper-motion and parallax information to refine the sample, and applied Gaussian mixture modeling to distinguish populations in proper-motion space. The resulting cluster-member candidates could then be examined separately from likely field and Small Magellanic Cloud contaminants.

From the selected stars, I examined velocity dispersion and the radial density distribution, then applied dynamical assumptions to estimate the cluster's mass. This project tied probabilistic classification to a physical measurement: changes to the member selection affect the quantities inferred from the sample.

05 / RESEARCH

Across the studies

These projects gave me repeated practice working with database queries, astronomical catalogs, uncertainty propagation, regression, statistical classification, and scientific figures. I also became more careful about explaining how a plotted result follows from its source data and selection criteria.
