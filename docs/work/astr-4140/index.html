01 / RESEARCH

Overview

In ASTR 4140, I carried out a series of research projects built around observational surveys and astrophysical simulations. The datasets and scientific questions changed between projects, but each required the same core practice: define a measurable question, select and clean the relevant data, construct an analysis, examine its limitations, and explain what the results mean physically.

02 / RESEARCH

Study I / Finding Sagittarius Stream candidates

Question: Can photometry and stellar-population models help distinguish likely Sagittarius tidal-stream stars from unrelated foreground and background stars?

The Sagittarius Stream is a remnant of a dwarf galaxy being disrupted by the Milky Way. I used photometric data from Sloan Digital Sky Survey Data Release 16 to select candidate stars in a region of the stream and assembled a comparison field for contrast.

I queried SDSS using spatial and photometric-quality criteria, corrected the stellar magnitudes for extinction, and propagated errors into the g−r color index. I then constructed color–magnitude diagrams for the candidate and comparison fields. To refine the selection, I compared the stars with a Dartmouth stellar-population isochrone using an age of 5 Gyr and metallicity [Fe/H] = −1.1, retaining candidates within a two-sigma tolerance around the model sequence.

The filtered color–magnitude diagrams and sky-density maps showed a more concentrated candidate population in the stream field than in the comparison field. The project taught me how much an astronomical conclusion depends on the steps taken before any final figure appears: database queries, quality cuts, extinction corrections, model assumptions, and the choice of control sample.

03 / RESEARCH

Study II / Black holes and their host galaxies

Question: How does a black hole's mass relate to the properties of the halo or galaxy that contains it, and how does the definition of the host change the observed relation?

I analyzed catalog data from the IllustrisTNG TNG100-1 simulation at snapshot 99, corresponding to redshift z = 0. Using Friends-of-Friends halo measurements and SubFind subhalo measurements, I compared black hole mass against host dark matter mass, stellar mass, and velocity dispersion.

For each comparison, I selected positive values for logarithmic analysis and fitted a power-law relation by applying linear regression in log–log space. The halo-scale relation gave a slope of approximately 1.39 with R² ≈ 0.86. The subhalo dark-matter relation was shallower and more dispersed, with a slope near 1.01 and R² ≈ 0.56. The subhalo stellar-mass relation had a slope near 0.77 and R² ≈ 0.83, while the velocity-dispersion relation showed a steeper slope near 3.95 and R² ≈ 0.85.

Comparing these fits made the importance of variable definitions and sample selection concrete. Measurements at halo and subhalo scales describe related physical systems through different quantities. The exercise also introduced me to the interpretation of fitted scaling relations and the numerical and modeling assumptions embedded in simulation catalogs.

04 / RESEARCH

Study III / Measuring the mass of NGC 362

Question: How can Gaia astrometry be used to identify members of a globular cluster and estimate its dynamical properties?

For this project I worked with Gaia Data Release 3 measurements around NGC 362, a globular cluster in the constellation Tucana. The challenge was to separate cluster stars from overlapping foreground and background populations before estimating physical quantities from their motions and spatial distribution.

I began with an ADQL query for sources around the cluster, then applied photometric and astrometric quality cuts, including a RUWE threshold of 1.4. I inspected color–magnitude and proper-motion diagrams, used proper-motion and parallax information to refine the sample, and applied Gaussian mixture modeling to distinguish populations in proper-motion space. The resulting cluster-member candidates could then be examined separately from likely field and Small Magellanic Cloud contaminants.

With the selected population, I studied velocity dispersion and the radial density profile, including an estimate of the cluster's tidal extent. The final analysis applied a virial-theorem model to connect dynamical measurements to a mass estimate. The work demonstrated how catalog cleaning, probabilistic classification, and dynamical assumptions all contribute to a single astrophysical measurement.

05 / RESEARCH

Across the three studies

These projects gave me repeated experience turning raw astronomical tables into physically interpretable figures. I worked with SQL and ADQL queries, numerical catalogs, uncertainty propagation, statistical models, and Python visualization. More importantly, each study required deciding what a dataset could support and how clearly to communicate that decision to someone reading the result.
