01 / INTERACTIVE

The rule

In BeLie, players explore a level and collect words at observation points. They combine those words in a Brain interface to form a belief statement. When the statement describes something false in the current world, believing it makes the statement true. One belief can remain active at a time, so choosing a new statement can remove the effect of the previous one.

For example, a statement about a bridge can change the player's route across a river. Its wording matters: placing a bridge somewhere in the level, directly in front of the player, or across the river can produce different spatial outcomes. We want players to discover this relationship through observation and experimentation.

02 / INTERACTIVE

Testing the idea on paper

I proposed the original concept for our CS 4730 project and helped develop its central mechanics with the team. We began with a physical prototype using a paper map, a movable bridge, word pieces, and BELIEVE / DISBELIEVE markers. The tabletop test let us examine where the player would collect words, how a sentence would be assembled, and how a changed belief might affect movement through the same space.

Wording changes placement

The paper writeup compares three ways to describe the same bridge:

“There is a bridge.”
It could appear somewhere unusable.
“There is a bridge in front of me.”
It would appear near the player, but might miss the river.
“There is a bridge across the river.”
It would span the gap and make a crossing possible.
03 / INTERACTIVE

Building the digital prototype

The team is developing the game in Godot. The current digital work includes terrain tiles, basic movement, map composition, and an early interface for collected words. I contribute to mechanics planning, level layout, character design, and terrain/environment design, including the relationship between platforming routes and the locations where words are discovered.

The next design problem is making belief changes legible to the player. Observations, word choices, and visible feedback all need to work together so that the player can understand why a statement changes the environment.
