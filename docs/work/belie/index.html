01 / INTERACTIVE

The central premise

The title BeLie comes from the idea that “the best part of believe is the lie.” The game begins with a rule about language: players explore a level, collect words through observations, and combine those words into a belief statement. When the statement describes something currently false, believing it can change the world to make it true.

A statement such as “There is a bridge in front of me” could create a new route across a gap. The design allows only one belief to remain active at a time. Replacing an existing belief can therefore undo one solution while creating another, turning the choice of statement into part of the puzzle.

02 / INTERACTIVE

Testing a language-based mechanic

I proposed the original premise for our CS 4730 team project and helped develop the core loop: explore, observe, collect words, compose a statement, and see how the world changes. Because the mechanic depends on both language and spatial reasoning, we began with a physical prototype before translating it into a digital level.

Our tabletop version used a paper map, word pieces, a movable bridge, and BELIEVE / DISBELIEVE markers. By changing the position of the bridge and the active statement, we could test how players understood the relationship between words and level geometry.

Wording changes placement

The paper writeup compares three ways to describe the same bridge:

“There is a bridge.”
It could appear somewhere unusable.
“There is a bridge in front of me.”
It would appear near the player, but might miss the river.
“There is a bridge across the river.”
It would span the gap and make a crossing possible.
03 / INTERACTIVE

From paper to Godot

The digital prototype uses Godot 4 and develops the same idea in a 2D environment. Current work includes a basic map, player movement, terrain tiles, and an early interface for collected words. The design plan also considers how bridges, visibility, illumination, and other environmental mechanisms can interact with belief statements as the prototype develops.

I contribute to the mechanics and the structure of the levels, as well as character and environmental design. My work on map and terrain layout connects the movement system to the locations where players will encounter words and test their beliefs.

04 / INTERACTIVE

Design direction

The central design question is how to make the rules of a language-driven world understandable through play. A useful puzzle should give players enough information to form a hypothesis, an opportunity to test it, and clear feedback when the world changes. We are developing the prototype around those moments of observation and revision.
