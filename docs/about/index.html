A Little About Me.

Curiosity has taken me to some interesting places.

Hi, I'm Lyra! I study Astronomy and Computer Science at the University of Virginia, where I'm also pursuing a minor in Philosophy. I like asking questions, following unexpected interests, and building things that take considerably longer than I first imagined.

My interest in astronomy began with childhood encyclopedias, science documentaries, and a film about Stephen Hawking that I watched when I was eight. I saw the Milky Way clearly for the first time during a family trip to Arxan, Inner Mongolia. A few years later, after watching the Perseid meteor shower from Hongcun, I decided to study astronomy. In high school, I helped run our school's observatory, organized observing sessions, and taught other students how to use telescopes.

I started making simple Scratch games in elementary school, but college was where I fell in love with building software. I enjoy the logic of programming and the creative process of making an idea work. Writing code gives me a pleasure I also find in writing fiction: I get to imagine something, work through its structure, and gradually make it real. My interests in game development, HCI, museums, and science communication have grown from two questions I ask whenever I learn something fascinating: What else can this teach me? And how can I share it with someone else?

Away from my desk, I write essays and fiction, take photographs, watch documentaries, play indie games, and spend an unreasonable amount of time looking at birds. Around UVA, I particularly enjoy the autumn squirrels, the groundhog near my residence hall, the crows nesting downstairs, the great blue heron fishing at Dell Pond, and the red-tailed hawks circling overhead in the late afternoon. A sparrow going about its day makes me just as happy. I love knowing that so much of the world carries on with its own concerns, quite independently of ours.

I'm also a feminist, a natural history enthusiast, and a fan of very large brunch omelets. My ideal free Saturday begins around lunchtime, includes some writing or a documentary, and ends with strength training and matcha Häagen-Dazs. I maintain that ice cream eaten after a workout has no calories, since it's cold. My scientific training has yet to convince me otherwise.
